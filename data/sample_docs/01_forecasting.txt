Electricity demand forecasting estimates future power consumption from historical patterns and external conditions. Demand often has strong daily and weekly cycles because human activity varies by hour and weekday. Useful engineered features include yesterday's demand at the same hour, last week's demand at the same hour, calendar variables, and historical rolling statistics. Weather, holidays, and missing data can matter as well. Always establish a simple seasonal baseline before training a complex model. Evaluate a forecast using data that occurs later in time than its training examples. MAE measures average absolute prediction error and is easy to interpret in the original units. When predicting a full next day, use only observations available at forecast creation time, not future measurements. Forecasting intervals help quantify uncertainty about point estimates.
